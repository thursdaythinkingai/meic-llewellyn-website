Y Llyfr · A trilogy of stories in one book

Beware your Dreams…

A very contemporary trilogy linking the visible world and the unseen, rooted in myth and the reality of life on a small mountain farm in west Wales. Join Gwinnie as she seeks to understand why she can suddenly fly, where her uncle disappears to at night, and where she really belongs.

Learn with her how to deliver lambs, how to talk to panthers, where Welsh fairies sleep, and when to turn yourself into a mouse. Share her coming-of-age journey, underground, overground and in deep water, before joining her in a struggle against dark and dangerous, all-too-human, adversaries.

“I was totally immersed from beginning to end… The author clearly has an intimate and sympathetic understanding of Welsh rural life, weaving Cymraeg beautifully into the story.”

David Elias, author of Shaping the Wild

Darn · An extract from Beware your Dreams…

Barn Owl

There is no such thing as silence. There is no such thing as dark. I can hear a beetle moving over fallen leaves on the forest floor below. I can hear the badgers snuffling through pine needles in the high wood, half a mile away. And the searchlight beam of my eyes, which only I can see, picks out a spider crawling up the bark below me, fixes on a sparrow slumbering in the ivy.

I am silence. I am darkness. And I am death. In a way, I am the perfect killer, coming from nowhere and never seen. I kill simply to eat, without pleasure or pity. It’s what I have to do. I have rested, now I float across the fields. In the thick gloom of dusk I am invisible, and my wings, almost motionless, make less sound than a moth’s.

* * *

But at last the ruined walls loom up before me, I swing up and through the empty window frame, and up again into the shadows of the massive roof. Here there is secrecy and silence and safety, here I will doze and dream, and stretch.

And then I will morph back into my human shape.

Yr Awdur · The author

Meic Llewellyn

Meic says he writes to ‘celebrate the culture of rural Wales’. But what is this culture? What does he mean?

“It’s music, poetry and language – all bound up together. And it belongs to everyone; we don’t have ‘cultured classes’ or ‘culture people’. Your bus driver today might be a harpist, the farmer over there is quite probably a bard, those teenagers on the corner could be dancing clogsian (clog dancing) this evening in the village hall, or singing in a choir.”

“The culture is something we all share in, our common possession. It’s our way of life. You see, we don’t have class distinctions here; ours is an egalitarian way of life; warm and welcoming, helping each other. That’s what I try to celebrate.”

Meic is the author of Beware your Dreams…, a trilogy of stories rooted in myth and life on a small mountain farm in west Wales. His next book, Stallion Girl, is set in west Wales in 1918.

Book two · Coming soon

Stallion Girl

Working title

West Wales, 1918

Not all the wandering stallion men were Roma, but many of them were. Not all the musicians and instrument makers were Roma, but most of them were. And not all the best horse-breeders, developing and perfecting the Welsh Cob, were Roma, but a good few were.

The Roma made up a vivid, elusive thread in the culture of this war-weary, impoverished and divided nation, and this story tells of a Roma teenager finding her own way, and finding herself in the process, at a time of intense crisis and desperate decisions.
