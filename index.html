Y Llyfr · A trilogy of stories in one book

Beware your Dreams…

A very contemporary trilogy linking the visible world and the unseen, rooted in myth and the reality of life on a small mountain farm in west Wales. Join Gwinnie as she seeks to understand why she can suddenly fly, where her uncle disappears to at night, and where she really belongs.

Learn with her how to deliver lambs, how to talk to panthers, where Welsh fairies sleep, and when to turn yourself into a mouse. Share her coming-of-age journey, underground, overground and in deep water, before joining her in a struggle against dark and dangerous, all-too-human, adversaries.

Themâu · Themes

Meic’s themes

The people, places, stories and ideas that live in these books.

Meic says he writes to ‘celebrate the culture of rural Wales’. But what is this culture? What does he mean?

“It’s music, poetry and the Welsh language – all bound up together and an integral part of our way of life. The culture belongs to everyone; we don’t have special ‘cultured classes’ or ‘cultured people’. Your bus driver today might be a fiddler or harpist, the farmer over there is quite possibly a bard, those teenagers on the corner could be dancing clogsian (clog dancing) this evening in the village hall, or singing in a choir. The culture is our common possession, the air we breathe.

“We’re predominately a Celtic people, and like all Celts feel close to the animal kingdom and the world of the spirit. Our farm animals are our families, the wild creatures our friends and confidants. And the otherworld is never very far away.

“That’s what I try to celebrate.”

Darn · An extract from Beware your Dreams…

Barn Owl

There is no such thing as silence. There is no such thing as dark. I can hear a beetle moving over fallen leaves on the forest floor below. I can hear the badgers snuffling through pine needles in the high wood, half a mile away. And the searchlight beam of my eyes, which only I can see, picks out a spider crawling up the bark below me, fixes on a sparrow slumbering in the ivy.

I am silence. I am darkness. And I am death. In a way, I am the perfect killer, coming from nowhere and never seen. I kill simply to eat, without pleasure or pity. It’s what I have to do. I have rested, now I float across the fields. In the thick gloom of dusk I am invisible, and my wings, almost motionless, make less sound than a moth’s.

* * *

But at last the ruined walls loom up before me, I swing up and through the empty window frame, and up again into the shadows of the massive roof. Here there is secrecy and silence and safety, here I will doze and dream, and stretch.

And then I will morph back into my human shape.

Yr Awdur · The author

Meic Llewellyn

Meic Llewellyn grew up in Aberteifi (Cardigan), west Wales, until moving with his family to London as a teenager. At school, his passions were drama, cricket and rugby. After university he returned to Wales to help his auntie on a farm in north Pembrokeshire before becoming a teacher. He taught History, English and Media in Wales, Scotland and England, then studied for a PhD at Aberystwyth University.

Since then he has been organising and co-ordinating cultural projects drawing together communities in the smaller languages of Europe. He has written poetry and told stories all his life, in both English and Welsh, but Beware your Dreams… is his first full-length book, written to celebrate the culture, wildlife and landscape of west Wales.

He lives on a smallholding in Bro Ddyfi with his wife Angela and a varied collection of animals, and is currently working on a second book, focusing on the Roma ‘stallion men’ who moved from farm to farm in Ceredigion over a hundred years ago.

Stallion Girl

Working title

West Wales, 1918

Not all the wandering stallion men were Roma, but many of them were. Not all the musicians and instrument makers were Roma, but most of them were. And not all the best horse-breeders, developing and perfecting the Welsh Cob, were Roma, but a good few were.

The Roma made up a vivid, elusive thread in the culture of this war-weary, impoverished and divided nation, and this story tells of a Roma teenager finding her own way, and finding herself in the process, at a time of intense crisis and desperate decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Meic Llewellyn?

Meic Llewellyn is a writer who grew up in Aberteifi (Cardigan) and lives on a smallholding in Bro Ddyfi. A former teacher with a PhD from Aberystwyth University, he has written poetry and told stories all his life, in English and Welsh. He writes to celebrate the culture of rural Wales: its music, poetry and the Welsh language, which belong to everyone.

What is Beware your Dreams… about?

Beware your Dreams… is a trilogy of stories in one book, linking the visible world and the unseen, rooted in myth and life on a small mountain farm in west Wales. It follows Gwinnie as she discovers she can fly, learns where her uncle disappears to at night, and searches for where she really belongs.

What is Stallion Girl?

Stallion Girl (working title) is Meic’s next book, a standalone story set in west Wales in 1918. It tells of a Roma teenager finding her own way, and finding herself, at a time of intense crisis and desperate decisions.
